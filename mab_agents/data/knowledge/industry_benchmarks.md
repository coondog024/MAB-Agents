# Collision industry benchmarks and what customers actually complain about

Compiled 2026-10-21 from CCC Crash Course (2025, Q4 2025, 2026, mid-year 2026),
J.D. Power Auto Claims Satisfaction Study (2022, 2025), Enterprise length of
rental (Q2 2026), CRASH Network Insurer Report Card (2026), the CCC "Moments of
Truth" study (2024), Collision Advice / CRASH Network "Who Pays for What?"
surveys, CIC post-repair inspection review (2024) and trade press. Use these to
set expectations with customers and to grade the shop against the industry;
quote the source when you use a number.

## What drives customer satisfaction

- A transparent, detailed explanation of what the car needs is the single
  biggest driver of satisfaction with the shop, ranking above speed (CCC
  Moments of Truth, 2,400 claimants, 2024).
- 88% want an update at least weekly; 45% expect one every 2 to 3 days.
  Satisfaction is 56 points higher (1,000-point scale) when updates arrive
  digitally, text highest (J.D. Power 2022). 29% of shops still update only
  when asked (2025 shop survey).
- An accurate time estimate up front is worth about 71 points; a repair that
  runs past three weeks without one drops satisfaction to 837 vs 908 (J.D.
  Power 2022). Track on-time delivery against the first date given.
- 42.5% of customers said the shop interaction changed how they viewed their
  insurer; 40.4% of total-loss customers switched carriers vs 7.6% after a
  repairable claim.
- 83% read Google reviews, 89% expect the owner to answer both positive and
  negative reviews (BrightLocal 2025).
- The first phone call: the most common complaint is a shop that opens with
  "Have you filed a claim yet?" Open with the person and the car ("Is everyone
  OK? Is the car drivable? Where is it now?"), insurance questions after. Up to
  21% of automotive service calls go unanswered (Marchex 2026); forward
  after-hours calls to a person and text back missed calls within minutes.

Shop practice that follows: a fixed update cadence every 2 to 3 days on the
customer's channel, every update ending with the next date they will hear from
us, bad news by phone, a date range until parts are in hand and the supplement
is approved, and re-promising proactively.

## Cycle time and rental

- Repairable cycle time claim-to-completion: 19.3 days (J.D. Power 2025, down
  from 22.3); 21.5 days for 2019+ vehicles with 3+ ADAS features vs 17.9 for
  pre-2015 vehicles without ADAS.
- Keys-to-keys: about 13 days with no calibration, 15.5 with one, 17+ with
  several (CCC Q1 2025 DRP data).
- Enterprise length of rental, U.S. collision, Q2 2026: 15.1 days overall,
  14.1 drivable, 20.7 non-drivable; rental reimbursement is commonly a fixed
  daily rate with a cap around 30 days. Flag at day 15 any RO likely to pass 25
  days; tell the customer before coverage ends.
- Legacy insurer assumption: about 4 billed hours per day of touch time;
  targets under 5 days drivable, under 10 non-drivable.

## Money

- Average total cost of repair $4,818 (CCC 2025 preliminary); average labor
  hours per repairable claim 26.7; 13.0 parts per appraisal.
- Deductibles of $1,000 or more: 26% of claimants (J.D. Power 2025), 28.4% of
  repairable collision claims (CCC mid-year 2026); 67% of policyholders would
  struggle with an unexpected $1,000 (VIU by HUB survey).
- Self-pay: 26% of repair orders in H1 2026, average ticket $1,941, 71% under
  $2,000. 7% of drivers avoided filing a claim for fear of a rate increase;
  44% had a rate increase in the past year.
- Betterment is limited in several states to wear items (tires, battery,
  brakes, exhaust); Maryland has no specific rule. Explain the prorated dollar
  amount and the measurement behind it before the part is ordered.

## Supplements and calibrations

- Average supplement grew from about $250 (2009) to over $1,060 (2024); 82% of
  repairs carried at least one supplement (CCC 2019); supplement frequency rose
  through 2025. Photo-based initial estimates were 26.4% of appraisals in 2025
  and run low by design.
- Calibrations on 28.3% of repairable estimates in 2025 (21.8% in 2024), 29.9%
  in Q1 2026, 40.6% at DRP shops; 51 to 56% of calibrations first appear on a
  supplement; 91% of scans are on the first estimate. BEVs average 1.70
  calibrations per estimate vs 1.54 for ICE (Mitchell 2025).
- Who Pays for What (2025-2026): about 90% of shops that ask are paid for
  pre-repair scans, but only 29% for in-process scans, 35% for steering-column
  inspection, 25% for destructive weld tests, 13% for bumper casting-line
  repair. Only 29% of shops are paid all or most of the time by State Farm for
  OEM-required safety inspections. Payment follows asking and documenting.
- No large national insurer scored above C+ on the 2026 CRASH Network Insurer
  Report Card (industry average C+, 680); keep a per-insurer response-time
  scoreboard and set expectations at intake from it. Washington now requires a
  written response within 5 business days of a supplement; Maryland has no
  deadline (Ins. §27-304 "failure to act promptly" only).

## Total loss

- Record 23.1% of claims totaled in 2025 (23.5% through Q2 2026); 11.2% of
  drivable and 52.5% of non-drivable vehicles; 74% of valuations are 7+ years
  old. J.D. Power puts total losses at 27% of claims with satisfaction down 9
  points and only 58% saying the valuation met expectations.
- Maryland timelines are in `maryland_collision_rules.md` (10 business days to
  offer, 7 to put the valuation in writing, 5 to answer a counter).

## Quality

- CIC post-repair inspection review (26 cases, Jan 2024): 90% later totaled,
  about 50% with unaddressed structural damage, 100% with finish defects, fewer
  than 30% calibrated, and in more than 80% the customer's first clue was paint
  (match, texture, overspray) or a headlamp. Delivery QC in daylight, the
  post-repair scan printout, and a 3-day follow-up call catch these first.

## Rates and margins

- Posted rate national averages, September 2026 (NABR LaborRateHero): body
  $85.56, refinish $84.65, frame $116.78, mechanical $161.63; CCC's paid-rate
  index rose only 2.9% in 2025, so posted rates outrun what carriers pay. No
  verified Maryland average is public; WMABA's regional survey is member-only.
- Classic gross-profit targets (consultant figures, dated): labor about 65%
  unloaded, parts 25% rising to about 34% with volume, paint and materials
  about 40% with P&M sales at 10%+ of sales, sublet 25%, whole job 40 to 45%.
  Boyd Group (Gerber) reported 46.4% gross margin for 2025.
- DRP scorecards (CCC) grade four quadrants: Estimating, Cycle Time, Quality,
  Customer Satisfaction; thresholds are in each carrier's agreement, so load the
  agreements into this folder.

## Roles a healthy small shop names an owner for

Front desk / CSR (capture rate, kept-informed score), estimator / repair
planner (supplement ratio, documentation), parts coordinator (parts cycle time,
return rate, parts GP), production manager (touch time, keys-to-keys, labor
efficiency), detail / QC (comebacks), accounts receivable and short-pay
follow-up (days sales outstanding, short-pay recovery), DRP compliance and
scorecards, reputation and CSI, training and renewals (I-CAR Gold Class roles
including the ADAS Technician required by the 2026 renewal for shops that
calibrate in-house). A five-person shop still needs each KPI owned by a name.
