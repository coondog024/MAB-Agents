# Front Desk Playbook (starter)

This is a starting template written for a collision shop front desk. Edit it to
match how Mullikin's actually works; the agents treat whatever is in this folder
as shop policy and will quote it to staff.

## The job in one paragraph

The front desk owns every customer touch that is not a wrench: the phone, the
check-in, the daily status texts, the insurer and rental phone calls, the
delivery and the payment. The goal is that no customer ever has to call us to
find out what is happening with their car.

## Daily routine

**Opening (first 30 minutes)**
1. Open the Today screen in MAB (`mab serve`) or run `mab brief`.
2. Work the "Needs an update" list first: every customer who has not heard from us in 2 days gets a text today.
3. Check "Ready for pickup": confirm pickup times and tell customers what they owe.
4. Check "Awaiting approval": a supplement with no answer after 48 hours gets a call to the adjuster; after 72 hours it goes to the manager. Give the customer the adjuster's name and claim number so they can push too.
5. Rentals: anyone past day 15, or within five days of their covered days, gets the adjuster asked for an extension today.
6. Confirm today's drop-offs and that a rental is arranged where the policy covers one.

**Through the day**
- Answer the phone by the third ring: "Thanks for calling Mullikin's Auto Body, this is <name>, how can I help?" A missed call gets a text back within a few minutes; after-hours calls forward to a person, not voicemail.
- Every stage change from the floor becomes a customer text the same day. Draft it in MAB, read it once, send it.
- Log every call or text on the RO (notes) so the next person can see it.

**Closing**
- Any car promised for tomorrow: confirm with production that it is on track, text the customer either way.
- Lock keys in the key box, mark which keys belong to cars that are ready.
- Deposit / batch out the card terminal.

## Phone scripts

### New claim / first call
Open with the person and the car, not the insurance: "Is everyone OK? Is the car drivable? Where is it right now?" The most common complaint about body shops is a first call that starts with "Have you filed a claim yet?"

Then ask: name and best mobile number; year, make, model; what happened and where the damage is; warning lights, leaks, wheel damage, airbag deployment (any of those means do not drive it); are they thinking of a claim or paying out of pocket (a claim number is not required to schedule); insurance company and claim number if they have one; does the policy have rental coverage, and for how many days and what daily limit; when can they bring it in.

Say: "You choose who repairs your car. If you tell the insurance company you are bringing it to Mullikin's, we handle the paperwork with them, including the estimate and any additional damage we find." Do not quote the law or say "in every state".

Do not promise a completion date on the phone. Say: "The insurance company's first estimate is a starting point; most jobs find more once the car is apart, and we document it with photos the same day. Once they approve the repair plan we will give you a target date and text you every two or three days."

Why the cadence matters: in CCC's study of 2,400 customers, a clear explanation of what the car needs mattered more than speed, 88% wanted an update at least weekly and 45% expected one every 2 to 3 days. Every update ends with when they will hear from us next. Bad news (a delay, hidden damage) goes by phone first, then text.

### Status call
Look the customer up by phone number in MAB. Read the current stage in plain words (the status text draft is already in plain words). If the car is waiting on the insurance company or a part, say so and say what we are doing about it. Then ask: "Would you like me to text you updates as it moves? We do that at every stage."

### Angry about a delay
Let them finish. Then: "You're right, that is longer than we want too. Here is exactly where it is today and the one thing it is waiting on. I am going to <action> and text you by <time>." Never blame the technician, the insurer or the parts vendor by name. If they want the manager, get the manager; do not say the manager is busy.

### "Why do I pay the deductible to you?"
"The insurance company subtracts your deductible from what they pay us, so it is collected by the shop at pickup. We take card, cash or check. If you believe the other driver was at fault, your insurance company may recover it for you later; that is a question for your adjuster."

### Insurance company calling
Get the claim number first, then the RO. Adjusters want: current status, whether the supplement is submitted, the promised date, and the rental return date. Never argue labor rates or operations on the phone; that is the estimator's or manager's conversation. Say: "Let me get <estimator> for you."

### Tow truck arrives
Get the tow bill and the driver's company name, photograph the car on the truck and after it is unloaded (all four corners), check for personal items and the key, write the RO as a tow-in, text the customer that it arrived.

### Rental
If the policy has rental: the carrier sets up the reservation; we give the rental company the promised date and update it when it moves. Write the covered days and daily limit on the RO at intake; coverage is usually a daily cap and a day cap, often 30 days, and the average non-drivable repair already uses about 21 rental days. The Today screen warns at day 15 and five days before the cap. Call the adjuster for an extension before the rental runs out, not after. Customer pay with no coverage: offer the rental company's shop rate if we have one.

### "Why is there a charge for tires or a battery?" (betterment)
"The insurance company only pays for the wear that was left on the old part. The new one gives you more life than you had, so they charge the difference." Show the dollar amount and the measurement behind it before the part is ordered; it is a separate line on the invoice from the deductible.

### "Should I file a claim or pay myself?"
About a quarter of repair orders are customer pay now. If the repair is near their deductible, offer a written cash estimate within 24 hours with no claim number required. Never predict what their premium will do; that is their agent's question.

### "It might be a total loss"
Only the carrier decides. In Maryland the title threshold is repairs over 75% of the car's value, but a carrier can total a car below that. Once they decide they generally have 10 business days to make a cash offer; the customer can ask in writing for the valuation details (7 business days) and counter (5 business days to respond). We send the total-loss notice the same day and copy the customer, put the storage policy in writing, and help with personal items, the lender payoff letter and the written valuation request. Rental days keep burning while they wait, so push the adjuster.

### After-hours pickup
Only if the balance is paid and a release is signed. Keys go in the lockbox; the code goes to the customer's mobile number on the RO only.

### "The insurer says I have to use their shop"
"You choose who repairs your car. The insurance company can recommend shops, but it has to tell you that you don't have to use them. They pay based on the estimate they approve, and if what they approve is less than our invoice we tell you before work starts, not at pickup. We work with <carrier> regularly." Then move on to scheduling. If they push, offer the manager.

Behind that script: Maryland Insurance Article 10-503 forbids an insurer from requiring a shop or recommending one without telling the customer they may choose, and from intimidating or coercing them. Write the adjuster's name, the date and the exact words on the RO. The Maryland Insurance Administration consumer line is 800-492-6116. Do not promise the insurer will pay our full invoice.

### "Is my car worth less now?" (diminished value)
"A repaired car can be worth less than before the accident; that's called diminished value, and in Maryland it's a real claim. If the other driver was at fault you claim it from their insurance company after the repair is done; after a hit-and-run or an uninsured driver your own uninsured-motorist property damage coverage has to consider it. Insurers don't bring it up, so you ask in writing, with a before-and-after valuation from a dealer or an independent appraiser. We'll give you the invoice, photos and reports for the file." Never quote an amount, never say we appraise or file it, and don't promise it applies under the customer's own collision coverage (the policy may exclude it). MIA Bulletin 24-8 and the Maryland rules doc have the detail.

## Paperwork (Maryland)

Four printed forms, all on the RO page under **Paperwork** (or `mab forms <ro>`):
the repair authorization with the "Customer's Rights" box at check-in; the replacement
crash parts list with the aftermarket statement before parts are ordered; the
additional-repairs consent whenever a supplement or customer-pay item is added; the
invoice addendum at pickup (used or rebuilt parts disclosed, replaced parts offered back,
balance, notices). The customer signs each one and the signed copy stays with the RO.

## Check-in checklist

1. Photo ID and insurance card; confirm phone number and preferred contact (text or call).
2. Signed repair authorization and direction-to-pay (we bill the insurer directly). The Maryland "Customer's Rights" block (Commercial Law 14-1008) sits right above the signature line, and the daily storage rate and its start date are written in. Tow-in or after-hours drop: give the rights notice by phone, then write down who was told, the date and time, and sign it.
3. Walk-around with the customer: photograph all four corners, VIN, odometer, dash lights, interior, and any prior damage; point prior damage out and note it on the RO.
4. Personal items out; toll transponder, garage opener and child seats noted.
5. Fuel level and mileage on the RO.
6. Explain the sequence: teardown, insurer approval of any additional damage, parts, body, paint, reassembly, scan and calibration, quality check.
7. Set expectations on updates: "You will get a text at every stage."
8. Rental handed off or confirmed.

## Delivery checklist

1. Car washed, interior vacuumed, no dust in jambs, no tape, no overspray on glass or trim.
2. Final invoice matches the approved estimate plus supplements; deductible and any customer-pay items itemized; used or reconditioned parts clearly marked as such (Commercial Law 14-1003); the customer signs and takes a copy.
2a. Offer the replaced parts back (14-1004; only parts that must go back to the manufacturer or distributor under a warranty agreement are excepted; a core-exchange part is still offered, the customer may keep it by paying the core charge) and note on the RO whether they took or declined them.
3. Walk-around with the customer at the repaired area and panel gaps; show the calibration report and post-repair scan if the job had them.
4. Warranty wording handed over and explained in one sentence, exactly as written in the shop profile (shop.toml `warranty`); do not improvise warranty terms.
5. Payment collected: card, cash or check. Insurer checks made out to the customer must be endorsed.
6. Keys, personal items, rental return reminder.
7. Review request text goes out the same day.

## Payment policy (template, edit to match the shop)

- Deductible is due at pickup. We do not "waive" deductibles. Anything the insurer declines (betterment on tires or batteries, operations they denied, upgrades the customer asked for) is told to the customer before the work is done and shows on the RO as a customer-pay item, never as a surprise at the counter.
- Customer-pay repairs: 50% deposit on parts over $500 before ordering; balance at pickup.
- Storage accrues daily on vehicles left more than 3 days after they are ready, and on total losses awaiting carrier pickup after the carrier is notified.
- Insurer-issued checks payable to the customer must be endorsed at pickup.

## Money talk before pickup (what the data says)

More than a quarter of claims now carry a $1,000+ deductible and two-thirds of
people would struggle to cover $1,000 unexpectedly. Nothing about money is heard
for the first time at the counter: the deductible, any betterment, anything the
insurer declined and the storage policy are told at drop-off and reconfirmed
two days before delivery. Maryland Commercial Law 14-1002: on customer-pay
work the written estimate cannot be exceeded by more than 10% without the
customer's consent.

## Escalate to the manager when

- A customer asks to speak to the manager.
- An adjuster denies a safety-related operation (scan, calibration, pretensioner) or asks the shop to skip an OEM procedure.
- A total-loss conversation starts.
- Any threat of a complaint to the insurance commissioner, the BBB or a lawyer.
- Any injury, fluid spill, or damage to a customer car in our care.
