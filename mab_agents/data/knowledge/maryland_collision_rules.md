# Maryland rules a collision shop and its front desk must get right

Compiled 2026-10-21 from the Maryland Code (mgaleg.maryland.gov), COMAR
(regs.maryland.gov), the Maryland Insurance Administration (MIA), the MVA, MDE
and federal rules. Each item names its source; the URLs are in `sources.toml`
so `mab kb fetch` keeps the originals in the knowledge folder. Re-verify each
year: the General Assembly meets every spring, and bills like SB 789 (2026) can
change this page.

Two corrections to things often repeated in the trade: Insurance Article
§27-306 is **not** the anti-steering law (it covers appeals of Commissioner
orders); the steering rule is §10-503. And the aftermarket crash-parts
disclosure is a duty on the **body shop** (Commercial Law §14-2302), not an
insurer OEM-parts mandate; Maryland has no insurer-side OEM parts law (bills in
2014, 2016 and 2020 all failed).

## The customer chooses the shop (anti-steering)

**Insurance Article §10-503** forbids an adjuster, appraiser, producer or insurer
employee from: recommending a specific repair shop *without informing the
claimant or insured that they do not have to use it*; requiring that an
appraisal or repair be made at a specific shop; requiring a specific contractor;
or intimidating, coercing or threatening a claimant into using one. §10-503(b)
bars adjusters from taking anything of value from a shop for referrals.
**§10-504**: civil penalty up to $1,000 per intentional violation plus license
action. **§10-502**: the insurer still need not pay more than the usual and
customary charges of similar shops in the area, and may require a drive-in
appraisal at its own facility.

The MIA's *Consumer Guide to Auto Insurance* (pp. 38-39) says the same in plain
words, and adds the part the front desk must not oversell: the insurer is not
required to pay for OEM parts unless the policy says so; "like kind and
quality" can include aftermarket and used parts; the customer may pay the
difference for OEM.

Front-desk use: "You choose who repairs your car. The insurance company can
recommend shops but must tell you that you don't have to use them. We work
with <carrier> regularly." If a customer reports lines like "they're not on our
list" or "we can't guarantee their work", write down the date, the adjuster's
name and the words on the RO, and give the customer the MIA consumer line,
**800-492-6116**. Sources: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gin&section=10-503 ;
https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/Documents/publications/autoinsuranceguide.pdf

## What the shop must put in writing (Commercial Law Title 14, Subtitle 10)

These apply to every "automotive repair facility" (§14-1001: anyone who
diagnoses or corrects malfunctions of a motor vehicle for profit, body shops
included). Enforcement is through the Consumer Protection Act and the Attorney
General's Consumer Protection Division (§14-1007; 410-528-8662). The MVA has no
jurisdiction over body shops and sends repair complaints to the AG.

**§14-1008, the "Customer's Rights" authorization.** Before any repair work the
shop must give the customer an authorization form that lists these rights
immediately above the signature line, in conspicuous type, under the heading
"Customer's Rights": (1) may request a written estimate for repairs over $50;
(2) may not be charged more than 10% over the written estimate without
consent; (3) is entitled to the return of replaced parts, except parts that
must go back to the manufacturer under warranty; (4) may not be charged for
repairs not originally authorized without consent. If the vehicle was towed in
or left while the shop was closed, oral notice is allowed, but the shop must
write down the name of the person notified, the date and time, and the
signature of the person who gave notice. The form also carries the federal
NHTSA "manufacturer special policy adjustment program" notice and the
premises-damage liability statement. This block belongs on the CRISMA repair
authorization printout.

**§14-1002, the written estimate.** For any job over $50 the shop must give a
written estimate on request before starting work (completion date, labor and
parts price, any surcharge, the premises-liability notice). The shop may not
charge more than 10% over that estimate without the customer's consent, and may
charge for preparing the estimate only if the customer was told in advance.
Missing the completion date is excused for acts of God, strikes, unexpected
illness or unexpected shortages of labor or parts.

**§14-1006, additional work.** No charge for repairs the customer did not
originally authorize unless the shop first got written or oral permission. For
every supplement log who approved it, when, and how (phone, text, email);
insurer approval alone is not customer consent.

**§14-1003, the final invoice.** Must describe all work and parts, including
warranty work, and must *clearly disclose* any used, rebuilt or reconditioned
part, including parts inside an assembly. It repeats the premises-liability and
NHTSA notices. After the customer signs, the shop gives the customer a copy and
keeps one (no retention period is stated; keep at least the 3-year Consumer
Protection Act window).

**§14-1004, replaced parts.** The shop must tender return of all replaced parts
except those that must go back under a warranty or exchange agreement. Offer
them at pickup and note on the RO whether the customer took or declined them;
an insurer's salvage request does not override the customer's right.

**§14-2302, aftermarket crash parts.** Before repairs, a body shop must give the
owner a list of the replacement crash parts and say whether each is genuine
(OEM or OEM-authorized). If aftermarket crash parts are used, the estimate must
carry, in 10-point capital letters: "THIS ESTIMATE HAS BEEN PREPARED BASED ON
THE USE OF AFTERMARKET CRASH PARTS THAT ARE NOT MANUFACTURED BY THE ORIGINAL
MANUFACTURER OF THE VEHICLE OR BY A MANUFACTURER AUTHORIZED BY THE ORIGINAL
MANUFACTURER TO USE ITS NAME OR TRADEMARK. THE USE OF CERTAIN AFTERMARKET CRASH
PARTS MAY MODIFY THE ORIGINAL MANUFACTURER'S WARRANTY ON THE CRASH PARTS BEING
REPLACED. UPON REQUEST OF THE CUSTOMER, THE BODY SHOP SHALL PROVIDE, IF
AVAILABLE, A COPY OF ANY WARRANTY FOR AN AFTERMARKET CRASH PART USED." "Crash
parts" (§14-2301) are the exterior and interior sheet-metal or fiberglass panels
that form the body or frame (fenders, bumpers, hoods, door panels) on passenger
cars. **Insurance Article §27-906** puts the parallel warranty-copy duty on
insurers.

Front-desk use: the parts-type column on the estimate is reviewed with the
customer and signed before parts are ordered; the OEM-upgrade difference is
quoted in dollars; the aftermarket statement goes with the estimate; LKQ and
reconditioned lines are marked on the invoice; replaced parts are offered at
delivery. The estimate auditor flags both the aftermarket and the used-parts
duties when the shop state is MD.
Sources: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-1008 ;
same URL with section=14-1002, 14-1003, 14-1004, 14-1006, 14-1007, 14-2302

## Storage charges and the garage keeper's lien

**Commercial Law §16-202(c)**: a shop holding a vehicle with the owner's consent
has a lien for repair, rebuilding, storage and parts from the moment the charges
are incurred. Storage supports a lien only when the owner asked for it, so the
MVA wants a signed storage agreement or written consent: put the daily storage
rate and its start date in the signed authorization. **§16-203**: the shop may
keep the vehicle until paid, and within 30 days after the lien is created must
notify every holder of a recorded security interest by certified or registered
mail. **§16-207**: after charges are unpaid for 30 days the shop may sell at
public sale between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m., but only after publishing notice once a
week for the two weeks before the sale and sending certified or registered mail
at least 10 days before the sale to the owner, every lienholder and the MVA;
proceeds go to sale costs, storage, the lien, secured parties, then the owner.
If the car is handed to a third-party storage lot, that holder's fees are
capped at $5/day or $300. The Court of Appeals (Allstate Lien & Recovery v.
Stansbury, 2015) held lien-enforcement costs cannot be added when the owner
redeems before sale.

The MVA's **form VR-100** (rev. 04-25) is the working checklist: pull the title
record (form DR-057) to find every owner and lienholder; send a separate
certified letter to each owner, each lienholder, the person who left the car and
the MVA Mechanic's Lien Unit (6601 Ritchie Hwy, Glen Burnie 21062) with year,
make, VIN and the auction time and place; advertise two consecutive weeks in a
paper covering the county; a licensed auctioneer is required in Anne Arundel
County, Baltimore County and Baltimore City; keep the postal receipts, the ad
and publisher's certification, a VIN photo and the signed work order or storage
agreement. Abandoned vehicles cannot be processed as mechanic's liens (call the
police); an owner who disputes the charges can file replevin in District Court,
which freezes the title. MVA 410-768-7000.

Shop practice: post the daily storage rate at intake and write it into the
authorization, start written notice on day one of an abandonment or total-loss
stall, never quote a lien-sale timeline shorter than 30 days unpaid plus the
10-day certified notice and the two-week ad.
Sources: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=16-202 ;
same URL with section=16-203 and 16-207 ; https://mva.maryland.gov/media/229

## Total loss

**Transportation Article §11-152**: a vehicle is "salvage" when the cost to
repair it for legal highway operation exceeds **75% of its pre-loss fair market
value** (cost figured per §13-506(c)(4), which excludes towing, storage, rental
and cosmetic repair), or when an insurer acquires it through a claim
settlement. 75% is the title threshold. **COMAR 31.15.12.02** defines the
insurer's total loss as repair cost that equals or exceeds the vehicle's actual
cash value *or a percentage of ACV set by the insurer*, and lets the insurer
count estimated hidden damage and anticipated rental, so a carrier can total a
car well below 75%. "Substantially similar" means same make and model, same
year or newer, same major options, similar or better condition, mileage within
the greater of 4,000 miles or 10%.

**COMAR 31.15.12.03-.06**: first-party cash offer within 10 business days of the
total-loss determination (30 days for an unrecovered theft; 10 days for a third
party); the offer is the retail value of a substantially similar vehicle from a
nationally recognized guide or a qualified dealer quote **plus taxes and
transfer fees**, whether or not the owner keeps the salvage; on written request
the insurer must give the offer, method, valuation math, deductions and
inspection guidelines in writing within 7 business days; the owner may counter
in writing and the insurer has 5 business days to respond. When a dispute
cannot be resolved, many policies offer an appraisal clause (check the policy).

**Titles.** An insurer that acquires a totaled vehicle must apply for a salvage
certificate within 10 days (Trans. §13-506). An owner who keeps the car files
VR-028 (owner retention); re-titling needs a Maryland State Police salvage
inspection plus a Title 23 safety inspection, and the new title is branded
"Rebuilt Salvage" (§13-507). Shops rebuilding owner-retained totals should keep
itemized parts receipts for the MSP inspection.

Front-desk use: say "this may total" before teardown when the estimate is near
the carrier's threshold; send the total-loss notice to the insurer the same day
and copy the customer; put the storage policy and the insurer's deadlines in
writing; help with personal items, the lien payoff letter and the written
valuation request. Only the carrier decides a total loss.
Sources: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=11-152 ;
same URL with section=13-506 and 13-507 ; https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/31.15.12.04 ;
https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/Pages/total-loss.aspx ; https://mva.maryland.gov/title-registration/title-vehicle/salvaged-vehicles

## Supplements, short pays and complaints

Maryland has no statutory response deadline for supplements and no MIA
guidance aimed at them. The hooks are the general claim-handling rules:
**Insurance §27-303** (single acts: misrepresenting facts or policy terms,
refusing to pay for an arbitrary or capricious reason, failing to explain a
denial promptly, bad faith on first-party claims), **§27-304** (business
practices: failing to acknowledge communications promptly, no reasonable
investigation standards, not attempting prompt fair settlement when liability
is clear), **§27-305** (penalties up to $2,500 per violation plus restitution)
and **COMAR 31.15.07**: acknowledge a claim within 15 working days, affirm or
deny coverage within 15 working days after proof of loss, and give a written
reason every 45 days while a first-party investigation stays open. **§10-502**
lets the insurer limit payment to usual and customary area charges.

Escalation path: document each short-paid line with the OEM procedure or DEG
inquiry number, invoice the balance, carry it as receivable (not a write-off),
follow up at 10 business days, then the customer letter and an MIA complaint.
The policyholder files (a shop may assist); third-party claimants may also
file. MIA Property & Casualty Complaints: online portal
enterprise.insurance.maryland.gov/consumer, email pccomplaints.mia@maryland.gov,
200 St. Paul Place, Suite 2700, Baltimore, MD 21202; 410-468-2000 or
800-492-6116; Rapid Response Program for auto claims without a formal
complaint, 410-468-2340. Allow up to 90 days for a decision. The MIA judges
whether the insurer acted reasonably; it does not set repair values.
Sources: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gin&section=27-303 ;
same URL with section=27-304 ; https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/31.15.07.03 ; https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/Pages/FileAComplaint.aspx

## Diminished value

Maryland treats loss of value after a repair as compensable third-party damage
(Frederick Motors v. Krausse, 1971; Berry v. Queen, 2020). **MIA Bulletin 24-8**
(April 12, 2024) adds that mandatory uninsured/underinsured motorist property
damage coverage must pay proven diminished-value claims and that policy
language excluding it under UMPD is unenforceable; optional first-party
collision coverage may still exclude it. The at-fault insurer need not
volunteer the claim, there is no required valuation method, and the claimant
carries the burden of proof (dealer before-and-after estimate or an
independent appraisal). The MIA consumer guide (pp. 42-43) covers it.

Front-desk script (`diminished_value`): a not-at-fault customer can claim it
from the at-fault carrier after repairs, or from their own UMPD after a
hit-and-run or uninsured driver; the shop supplies the invoice, photos and
reports; the shop does not appraise or file it, and never quotes an amount.
Source: https://insurance.maryland.gov/Insurer/Documents/bulletins/24-8-Statutorily-Required-Uninsured-Underinsured-Coverage-for-Value-Damages.pdf

## Labor rates

There is no Maryland statute, regulation or MIA survey that sets or publishes a
prevailing labor rate (a 2007 bill to create one, HB 1084, never passed).
Insurers set "market" rates from their own DRP data. The only published
regional benchmark is the WMABA Labor Rate Survey (Baltimore, Annapolis,
Hagerstown, DC, Northern Virginia regions; last run 2023, results in Hammer &
Dolly). Treat third-party sites quoting a "Maryland average" as unverified. The
shop's leverage is its posted rate, documented and applied to every customer,
the customer's right of choice (§10-503) and the complaint path above.
Source: https://wmaba.com/labor-rate-survey/

## Rental coverage

The MIA guide describes rental reimbursement as usually a fixed daily rate up to
a maximum number of days, "usually not to exceed 30 days". Capture the daily
limit and the day cap at intake; days past the cap are the customer's money.

## Sales tax on the invoice

**COMAR 03.06.01.03**: labor that restores existing property (collision repair)
is not subject to the 6% sales and use tax. Parts and materials incorporated
into the vehicle are taxable when separately stated, and the shop buys them
under a resale certificate. If the shop bills one lump sum for parts and labor,
no part of the charge is taxed but the shop loses the resale exclusion and owes
tax on the parts it bought. Shop supplies and consumables that are not
transferred to the customer are taxable to the shop. The Comptroller's Business
Tax Tip #7 applies the same rules to auto repair. Keep parts and separately
stated paint and materials taxable, labor and sublet labor exempt, and confirm
the paint-and-materials treatment with the Comptroller.
Source: https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/03.06.01.03

## Licensing

Maryland has no state body-shop or automotive-repair-facility license. The
MVA licenses dealers, dismantlers, inspection stations, tow operators and
driving schools, not body shops; a shop that also does Maryland safety
inspections holds an MVA inspection-station license (COMAR 11.14). The
consumer-protection duties above are the only state-level rules. County
business registrations may still apply.

## Paint shop and waste (federal plus Maryland)

- **EPA 6H, 40 CFR 63 Subpart HHHHHH**: applies to any area source that
  spray-applies coatings to motor vehicles or mobile equipment (a shop that
  sprays no coatings containing chromium, lead, manganese, nickel or cadmium
  compounds can file a notification of non-use instead). Spraying only in a
  fully enclosed booth or prep station at negative pressure with filters rated
  at least 98% capture; HVLP, electrostatic, airless or equivalent guns;
  enclosed gun cleaning; every painter trained and certified with retraining
  at least every 5 years; initial notification and notification of compliance
  status (now filed through EPA's CEDRI); records kept 5 years. Sources:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-63/subpart-HHHHHH ;
  https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/63.11170
- **COMAR 26.11.19.23, Vehicle Refinishing** (Maryland's VOC rule, effective
  July 1, 2013): VOC limits as applied in g/L: adhesion promoter 540,
  pretreatment 660, primer 250, clear 250, color/basecoat 420, multicolor 680,
  single-stage 340, temporary protective 60, truck bed liner 200, underbody 430,
  other 250; cleaning solvents other than bug-and-tar removers 25 g/L. HVLP or
  equivalent application; enclosed gun cleaning; closed containers for
  coatings, solvents and wet rags; trained applicators. **Records for 3 years**:
  a current list of every coating and solvent with mix ratio and actual vs.
  allowed VOC, plus supplier data sheets and purchase records. Paint-company
  "Maryland VOC charts" track this table. Source: https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/26.11.19.23
- **MDE Air Quality General Permit to Construct for Vehicle Refinishing** sits
  on top of 6H: fixed location, no more than 400 gallons of refinishing
  materials a year, two or fewer booths, no lead or formaldehyde coatings;
  $500 fee; painter certification within 180 days of hire; an Annual
  Notification of Changes Report by March 1 each year; 5-year records. The MDE
  package found is dated June 2013; confirm the current version and fee with
  MDE's Air and Radiation Administration. Source:
  https://mde.maryland.gov/programs/Permits/AirManagementPermits/Documents/00_Autobody_Package%20Copy%206-13.pdf
- **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.134**: written respiratory-protection program with a named
  administrator, medical evaluation, fit testing before use and annually,
  training; OSHA interpretation letters treat supplied-air respirators as the
  acceptable protection for spraying isocyanate clearcoats. HazCom (SDS binder,
  labelling) is the other routinely cited standard.
- **Hazardous waste, COMAR 26.13**: Maryland does not adopt the federal VSQG
  exemption. A shop generating under 100 kg (about 220 lb) a month and never
  holding more than 100 kg is a Maryland-defined small quantity generator
  (COMAR 26.13.02.05): it must still determine which wastes are hazardous
  (gun-wash solvent, paint sludge, solvent rags, aerosols, thinner), ship them
  to a permitted facility and never landfill liquids. Hitting 100 kg in any
  month makes it a fully regulated generator under COMAR 26.13.03: EPA ID,
  manifests, labelled containers, 90-day accumulation limit, training,
  contingency plan, 3-year records. MDE's 2021 compliance guide explains the
  May 3, 2021 reorganization; MDE Resource Management Program 410-537-3315.
  Used oil and antifreeze follow separate recycling rules. Sources:
  https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/26.13.02.05 ;
  https://mde.maryland.gov/programs/land/HazardousWaste/Documents/Compliance%20Guide%20-%20MD%20HW%20generators%20-%20new%20requirements%20eff%205-3-21%20FNL.pdf

Compliance calendar to keep (an owner should hold these dates): each painter's
6H training date plus five years; fit-test and medical-clearance dates per
painter; filter change log; MDE Annual Notification of Changes by March 1;
booth permit number; the 3-year VOC coatings list and purchase records; waste
manifests and generator ID; spill-kit inspections.

## Pending

**SB 789 (2026 session)** as introduced would have required MVA licensing of any
repair facility performing ADAS recalibration (background check, bond, fee,
penalties up to $5,000), mandated OEM procedures with pre- and post-repair
scans, written pre- and post-repair ADAS disclosures to the customer, and
scan/calibration line items on insurer estimates. The Senate amended it into an
MVA study of ADAS repair and training requirements with a report due January 1,
2027 (passed the Senate 46-0; the bill page showed a House Rules referral and no
chapter number, so enactment is unconfirmed). The fiscal note confirms the only
current law for repair facilities is Commercial Law Title 14, Subtitle 10 plus
§14-1327 (ADAS recalibration disclosures for glass shops). Producing the
pre/post ADAS disclosure documents now costs nothing and matches the OEM
position statements the shop already follows.
Source: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/fnotes/bil_0009/sb0789.pdf
